# Short-Form Video Platforms, Sustainability Content, and Purchase Intention Among Generation Z

**A Cross-Platform Analysis of TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts**

**Authors:** Demeshwar Rana and Anshul
**Affiliation:** MSc Data Science, AI and Digital Business, GISMA University of Applied Sciences, Potsdam, Germany
**Paper:** [575_Rana.pdf](575_Rana.pdf)
**Keywords:** Generation Z, sustainability content, short-form video, purchase intention, TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, S-O-R framework, PLS-SEM, SDG 12

## Summary

This study looks at how seeing sustainability content on short-form video platforms relates to Generation Z's intention to buy sustainable products. It uses the Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) framework and tests three mediators (environmental awareness, environmental attitude and perceived social norms) with PLS-SEM on a survey of 100 international students in Germany.

## Research Problem

Generation Z grows up with sustainability messages, and short-form video algorithms keep pushing such content to them. It is still poorly understood how this daily exposure turns into an intention to buy sustainable products.

## Research Question

> How does exposure to sustainability-related content on short-form video platforms affect purchase intention among Generation Z consumers?

## Research Gap

- Most earlier work treats "social media" as one block and does not separate out short-form video.
- Existing short-form video studies cover a single platform, usually TikTok, and often one country or one product category.
- No study found compares TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts together for Generation Z with sustainability content as the main variable. A systematic review of 92 studies points to the same gap.

## Conceptual Model and Hypotheses

Sustainability content exposure (SCE, the stimulus) leads to three internal states (the organism), which lead to sustainable purchase intention (PI, the response).

| Hypothesis | Path |
|---|---|
| H1 | SCE to Environmental Awareness (EA) |
| H2 | SCE to Environmental Attitude (AT) |
| H3 | SCE to Perceived Social Norms (SN) |
| H4 | EA to PI |
| H5 | AT to PI |
| H6 | SN to PI |

## Method

- **Design:** quantitative, cross-sectional online survey (Google Forms)
- **Sample:** 100 Generation Z students from four German universities, recruited by convenience sampling (79% postgraduate, 51% mainly see sustainability content on Instagram)
- **Instrument:** 25 items, 5 per construct, on a 5-point agreement scale, adapted from earlier studies
- **Analysis:** PLS-SEM in SmartPLS with 5,000 bootstrap subsamples, plus PLSpredict, HTMT, VIF, Harman's single factor test and an exploratory platform comparison (Kruskal-Wallis, Spearman)

## Results

| Hypothesis | Path | Beta | p | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | SCE to EA | 0.901 | < 0.001 | Supported |
| H2 | SCE to AT | 0.854 | < 0.001 | Supported |
| H3 | SCE to SN | 0.719 | < 0.001 | Supported |
| H4 | EA to PI | -0.201 | 0.109 | Not supported |
| H5 | AT to PI | 0.391 | 0.002 | Supported |
| H6 | SN to PI | 0.763 | < 0.001 | Supported |

- Five of six hypotheses are supported. The model explains **84.1%** of the variance in purchase intention, and Q2 predict is positive for all endogenous indicators.
- **Perceived social norms are the strongest predictor** of purchase intention (f2 = 1.792), then attitude. Awareness has no significant effect.
- Exposure relates to purchase intention through social norms (indirect beta = 0.549) and attitude (0.334), but not through awareness.
- **Platform comparison (exploratory):** no significant differences in construct scores between Instagram, YouTube Shorts and TikTok users, and exposure is positively correlated with purchase intention on all three platforms.

## What the Study Contributes

- Examines the exposure to purchase intention pathway on short-form video specifically, with three mediators in one model.
- Suggests that peer norms, not awareness, link sustainability content to purchase intention. For brands and communicators, content that shows peers choosing sustainable products may work better than content that only informs.
- Links to SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption and Production) and SDG 13 (Climate Action).

## Limitations

- Convenience sample of 100, mostly postgraduate students from four German universities, so results do not generalise widely.
- Only 17 TikTok respondents, so platform comparisons are exploratory and cannot show that one platform is more effective.
- Discriminant validity is not fully established: four construct pairs have HTMT above 0.90, and EA and AT are highly collinear (VIF above 5), so the negative EA to PI path should be read with caution.
- Single self-reported questionnaire; Harman's test (53.2%) and full collinearity VIFs mean common method variance cannot be ruled out.
- Cross-sectional design measuring intention, not actual purchases, so the findings are associations, not causal effects.

## Future Work

- Larger, cross-national and more diverse samples.
- Measure platform-specific exposure and actual purchase behaviour, not only intention.
- Refine the items so awareness, attitude and exposure are empirically distinct.
- Confirm the social norms result with items that separate peer expectations from personal intention.
- Run formal multi-group analysis across platforms with larger subgroups.
